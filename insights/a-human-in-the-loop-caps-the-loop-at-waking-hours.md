Most companies experienced ChatGPT as a question-answer bot and then graduated to longer-running agents that go away, get stuck, and come back. Blomfield's observation is that this shape makes the human "the gating mechanism": if the agent stops halfway through at three in the morning waiting on input, it cannot go anywhere. The productivity framing — engineers 20% more productive, copilots for lawyers — hides the structural fact that every one of those gains still runs at human speed, because a person sits in the path of every decision.

The prescription follows directly: if you can close the loop without a human, the product improves itself while you sleep. This is why [[make-the-quality-gate-a-second-adversarial-model-not-a-human]] is load-bearing rather than a cost optimisation — the human gate is the thing that has to go for [[a-learning-mechanism-is-what-separates-a-loop-from-a-pipeline]] to matter. It sharpens [[reorganize-around-agents-dont-bolt-them-onto-old-workflows]] with a concrete diagnostic: ask where in your system a person is holding the baton overnight. Note the deliberate tension with [[supervise-loops-from-a-leveraged-point-not-from-inside-them]] — both agree the human should not sit *inside* the loop, but disagree on how much supervision the leveraged point still owes.

Thariq Shihipar contrasts his prior startup experience against Anthropic's culture: "in my previous company, I was very used to being reasonable — I'd write down this list of priorities and be like, well, I guess we can prioritize this against this... this will be our priority this quarter. But what if you just did all of it? What if you forced reality to show you the tradeoff?" The claim isn't that tradeoffs don't exist in the end — it's that most "tradeoffs" people plan around are self-imposed assumptions made before the constraint was ever tested, and agent-era capability increases mean many of those assumed constraints have quietly stopped binding: "good, fast, cheap — now it's pick three."

The operational form of this is refusing to pre-ration ambition: "I think the best way to do more ambitious work is to reframe and make ourselves more ambitious, because I think the only way to prove that agents work is to do the best work of our lives faster than ever before." This is a sharper, more falsifiable version of the general instinct behind [[choose-problems-backward-from-an-outcome-you-want]] — reasoning backward from an outcome you actually want rather than the constraint set you've assumed — applied specifically to organizational prioritization rather than research problem choice. It pairs directly with [[building-is-easier-but-generating-value-is-still-hard]]: attempting everything only pays off if what you build after removing the assumed constraint is actually valuable, not just more output.

Blomfield borrows Jack Dorsey's framing explicitly: in a conventional company intelligence is spread throughout the people and the hierarchy routes it; in the new model intelligence lives in the system, and people live around the edge where that intelligence makes contact with reality. Dorsey's list of what stays human is specific — reaching into places the model cannot yet go, sensing what it cannot perceive, intuition, opinionated direction, cultural context, trust dynamics, the feeling in a room — plus the calls the model should not make on its own: ethical decisions, novel situations, and high-stakes moments where the cost of being wrong is existential.

The load-bearing line is that a world model which cannot touch the real world is just a database. Humans remain in the sales call, in the client's office, convincing the CEO and pitching the investor; what they stop doing is routing information up and down layers of management for approval. This is the positive half of the argument whose negative half is [[a-human-in-the-loop-caps-the-loop-at-waking-hours]] — remove people from the conduit, keep them at the boundary. It gives a shape to the reorganisation demanded by [[reorganize-around-agents-dont-bolt-them-onto-old-workflows]], and it is the same division of labour as [[ai-compresses-execution-not-judgment]] and [[dissolved-constraints-shift-bottleneck-to-judgment]] drawn at the level of an org chart rather than an individual.

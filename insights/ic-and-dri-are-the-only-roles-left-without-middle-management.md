If humans are no longer the conduit for information moving up and down a hierarchy, the layer whose whole job was that conduit has no remaining function. Blomfield says it plainly: no middle management, no layers of directors and VPs. Two roles survive. The individual contributor — everyone actually doing the work, and turning up to meetings with working prototypes rather than decks. And the DRI, a single person whose head is on the block for an outcome, which he calls the biggest thing most companies are missing. His counter-example is from working at a bank: a committee for everything, and it grinds things to a halt.

The claim only holds given the substrate — it is a consequence of [[humans-move-to-the-edge-where-the-model-touches-reality]] and of [[if-it-left-no-artifact-it-didnt-happen]], because routing can only move into the system once the system can read everything. The prototype-not-deck standard is the org-level expression of [[ai-compresses-execution-not-judgment]]: when building a working version is cheap, presenting a description of one stops being evidence of anything. Read alongside [[token-spend-replaces-headcount-as-scaling-unit]], which supplies the budget mechanics, and [[organizational-shape-is-the-emerging-moat-in-ai]], which argues the resulting shape is itself defensible. Worth holding against [[institutional-slowness-is-the-runway-not-the-threat]] — the committees that grind things to a halt are exactly the drag that gives newcomers their window.

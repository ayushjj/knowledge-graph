Of the five steps Tan gives the audience, he singles this one out as "the discipline that separates the compounders from the dabblers." Most people run a single operation with an agent and then throw the context away — they close the window, and that is it. The correction is a habit applied at the end of every task: ask the agent to turn what it just did into a markdown file you can reuse forever. His compressed version of the rule is the one he says he uses at YC: "If you have to ask for something twice, you failed."

The framing that makes it stick is what the alternative actually is. "The person who captures what they learn gets smarter every single day. The person who wakes up every morning with amnesia" is wasting the time, and — the load-bearing half — "it sort of doesn't matter how good the model gets if you can't turn it into real memory." That last clause is why this is not merely a productivity tip: it is the mechanism by which a rented model becomes an owned asset, per [[personal-agi-is-an-asset-you-build-not-a-subscription-you-rent]], and the per-task action whose accumulated output is the thing at stake in [[own-your-skill-files-or-your-job-becomes-one]]. It is the individual-scale version of [[compound-engineering-makes-future-work-easier]], and it inherits the caveat from [[being-worth-retrieving-from-is-the-product]] — skillifying indiscriminately builds the garbage dump, so capture needs pruning as its other half.
